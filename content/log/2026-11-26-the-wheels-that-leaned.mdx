The [household robot](/builds/household-robot) sat level on Sunday. By Monday evening its
wheels were leaning in.

## Why the cradles rolled

Each wheel servo sat in its own printed cradle with two bolts through the foot. Both bolts sat
on one line, 11 mm from the foot's inboard edge, while the tire pushed 53 mm out. That is a lever
of 4.7 to 1. The cradle rolled about the edge of its foot, the servo's outer end lifted, and the
wheel leaned. The cradle's cheeks also took the bending across their print layers, where a
printed part is weakest.

A new rule went into the checklist every printed part goes through: trace the load path from the
tire to the plate before modelling anything.

## Wheel module v2

The fix is one PETG piece that boxes both servos and ties them together with an axle beam,
bolted down with 15 M3 screws. Every check runs at five times the static load, with the planned
arm reaching forward: 73 N on each wheel.

- The lever dropped from 4.7 to 1.6 to 1.
- The worst bolt carries 57 N, and the walls see about 0.5 MPa.
- An 18 g test piece checked the servo fit before the 164 g module printed.

The plan included a new base plate to match. It would only have moved 15 holes, so the old plate
was drilled instead, with the module as its template, and the filament was saved.

A crack then showed through one servo box's top wall, along a layer joint. It was stitched with
steel wire sunk in with the soldering iron and sealed with a melted bead, and the robot passed a
hands-on load check. A 6806 bearing on each wheel, so the servo only drives and the box carries
the weight, comes before the arm.

## The electronics deck

Three printed trays now sit on the plate.

- **Compute tray.** The Raspberry Pi 5 with its cooler, and a 5 V converter that feeds it.
- **Servo bridge.** A small table over the caster that holds the board driving the wheel servos.
- **Power tray.** A 12 V converter for the servos, a current and voltage sensor that will act
  as the fuel gauge, tie points for the fuses, and a panel with the main switch and the charge
  socket.

A fit coupon settled the switch cutout and the standoff sizes before the trays printed. Two
lessons from assembly: zip ties have to be threaded before a tray is bolted down, and only the
bridge's legs hold captive nuts.
