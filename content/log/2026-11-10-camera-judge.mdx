Experiment 0 needs a person to watch every trial and type y or n. Experiment 1 replaces
that person with a $60 webcam, because everything after it (a motion model, then a small
reinforcement learning experiment) needs an automatic judge.

## What got built

- **Interlocks in the safety gate.** Rules that depend on the world, not the request: no
  recent sighting of the robot, no motion; a move whose predicted end comes too close to
  the arena edge is refused. They live in the gate, logged like every other rule, so the
  camera only ever reports where things are.
- **A tracker that works on a busy floor.** The arena is six puzzle mats, two of them the
  same orange as the robot's stickers. Colour tracking couldn't work, so the robot is
  whatever differs from a picture of the empty arena, and stickers are only read *inside*
  its outline. Shadows are ignored by counting colour change separately from brightness.
- **A heading that doesn't need careful stickers.** One forward move measures the angle
  between where the stickers point and where the robot actually goes (28 degrees here).
- **A simulator** of the robot and the overhead camera, so every piece runs in tests.
- **Privacy by default.** Frames are tracked and dropped; only numbers are logged. The two
  commands that save an image refuse unless told nobody is in view.

## What the first night taught

- Tracking a still robot: steady to a millimetre and a degree, in every frame.
- One forward half-second: 6.4 cm. Short pulses (0.3 s) barely move it.
- The safety fence did its job: 21 moves refused for the edge, 6 for lost tracking. It
  was also too cautious for a 61 x 41 cm arena until it got measured speeds.
- Two things broke the empty-arena picture: the webcam re-balancing its own exposure when
  a dark robot entered view, and the mats sliding. The first is now corrected in software.
  The second needs tape.
- One mistake worth recording: a calibration snapshot came from the wrong camera (macOS
  and OpenCV number cameras differently) and caught people. It was deleted at once, and
  snapshots now open the camera by name.

Next session: tape the mats, retake the background, collect thirty moves, fit the motion
model. Then the question this experiment exists for: how often does the camera agree with
a person?
