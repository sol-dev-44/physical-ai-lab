## The run

The bowl dataset grew to 40 episodes, about 35,000 frames. Episode 0 was a failed
demonstration and is left out. ACT then trained for 50,000 steps on episodes 1 to 39, on
the Mac's GPU at about 1.7 steps a second: 7 hours 44 minutes, a checkpoint every 5,000
steps.

## On the arm

The first 30-second run reached the Lego, bumped it on the way down, backed off,
regrasped, dropped it in the bowl and went back to rest. Shaky, but it did the whole task.

Then the placements varied:

| Lego placement | What happened |
|---|---|
| Flat, horizontal | Once grabbed right away, twice missed or overshot |
| Flat, stacked on two other blocks | Grabbed on the first try |
| Vertical | Missed three times |
| Rotated to 12 o'clock | Gripper didn't rotate to match; missed three times beside it |

The pattern is the training data. The demonstrations were mostly flat placements, and
those are what the policy can grasp. Rotated and vertical ones are barely in the data.

## Changing how it runs

ACT predicts a chunk of 100 future actions at a time. Two settings change how those are
used, without retraining:

- **10 actions per chunk:** the arm barely moved and shook periodically. Worse.
- **Temporal ensembling** (a new chunk every step, overlapping predictions averaged): the
  control loop held 30 Hz instead of dipping to about 16, and the motion was smoother. It
  still missed twice to the left, under-rolled the wrist, and hovered.

Neither changes what the policy knows. Smoother isn't better at grasping.

<Callout>These nine runs were exploratory, one setting at a time, with no fixed placement list. They aren't a success rate. Each is recorded as a row in `experiments/exp002-so-arm-bench/trials.md`.</Callout>
