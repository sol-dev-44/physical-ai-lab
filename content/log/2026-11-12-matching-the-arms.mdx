Day two on the SO-ARM101 bench. The goal was a calibration good enough to record
demonstrations from, then a first learned policy, however rough.

## The 90-degree gripper

In teleop the follower's jaws pointed about 90 degrees away from the leader's. The cause
was the calibration's middle pose. Wrist roll is a full-turn joint, so calibration records
it as the whole 0 to 4095 turn and takes its zero only from the pose at the middle step.
The two arms had been put in that pose with their wrists turned differently.

The fix: at the middle step, put both wrist rolls at the same physical orientation, jaws
pointing the same way. After that recalibration, shoulder lift, elbow, wrist flex and
wrist roll all tracked the leader smoothly.

## A wrist that turns back

A full roll of the leader's wrist makes the follower stop halfway and reverse. The raw
reading wraps from 4095 to 0, so the goal jumps by nearly a full turn the other way, and
the speed cap walks the follower toward it the long way round. For now the leader's wrist
stays within about 150 degrees of the middle. The real fix, a wrap-aware speed cap that
picks the nearest equivalent angle, belongs in the lab harness, not the bench.

## Camera rules first

Imitation learning needs camera frames, and the operator's hands are in them. Two
decisions went in before any recording:

- **ADR 0009:** arm bench frames may show adults, including the operator. Never a child.
- **ADR 0010:** for this experiment only, recordings may go to an external GPU for training,
  with strict conditions: a named provider, encrypted transfer, every episode checked for
  children first, and the provider's copy deleted after each run. Raw frames are never
  published or committed.

No upload has happened yet.

## The first policy

Five demonstrations, then 5,000 steps of ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers) on the
Mac. On the arm it moved, but never reached down to the Lego.

The demonstrations explained why. Wrist flex was commanded up to 117 degrees, but the
follower only reached about 89.5. In 1,445 of the 4,500 recorded frames the command was
past what the follower could reach. Wrist roll and elbow showed the same gap, smaller.
The policy learned targets the arm can't follow, so it stalled near the edge of its range.

## Next

The problem is data and reach, not hardware: recalibrate both arms within the follower's
reachable range, add an overhead camera and a wrist camera, then record 30 to 50
demonstrations with the Lego in different positions and orientations.
