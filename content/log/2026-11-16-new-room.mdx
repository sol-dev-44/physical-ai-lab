Last night the bench moved: a new room, new lighting, the cameras, arms and bowl all in new
places. Nothing was tightly controlled, on purpose. A policy that only works on one table
isn't much use.

## It failed in the new room

The second policy, trained on the 64 episodes recorded in the old setup, went 0 for 4 in the
new one. That's the expected result. Every demonstration it had seen came from a scene that
no longer exists.

## A better camera, checked by numbers

A Logitech C920 replaced the old overhead webcam. It was aimed with a live view that writes
nothing, then checked with a script that prints only numbers from 5 seconds of frames:
brightness 125, no drift, under 1% blown out, nothing crushed to black, and sharpness 639 with
almost no change between frames (so autofocus wasn't hunting). No frames were saved.

## 30 demonstrations in the new room

Three blocks of 10, with the bowl in a different spot and the lighting changed for each
block. Each block mixed placements: 3 flat, 2 vertical, 3 rotated, 1 stacked, 1 near an edge
or the bowl. Then a third policy (v3) trained from scratch on all 94 good episodes, about
8 hours on the Mac.

## The fixed test

Nine runs, the same nine every time: three bowl spots, each with the Lego flat-horizontal,
vertical (long side pointing away from the arm) and rotated to about 1 o'clock. The Lego
always lies flat. It was reset by hand between runs.

| Placement | Grasped | In the bowl |
|---|---|---|
| Flat-horizontal | 2 of 3 | 1 of 3 (one released too high and bounced off) |
| Vertical | 1 of 3 | 1 of 3 |
| Rotated | 0 of 3 | 0 of 3 |

Two runs were perfect from start to finish. Most misses had the height right but landed 2 to
4 inches off on the mat. On every rotated block the wrist didn't turn far enough, the same
weakness the first policy had.

<Callout>
  Nine trials can't separate 2 of 9 from 4 of 9. The 95% interval on 2 of 9 runs from about
  3% to 60%. The test is for direction, and for seeing how runs fail.
</Callout>

## The next batch

30 more demonstrations weighted toward what failed: per block, 6 angled (three each way), 2
vertical and 2 flat. In these demos the wrist turns to match before the arm goes down, the
final approach is slow, and the Lego is released low over the bowl. A fourth policy (v4) on
all 124 good episodes trained overnight.
