The soldering and wiring tools for the household robot's harness arrived with no home on the
bench, so the lab printed one. Tessera is a set of storage modules, modelled in parametric CAD
like the robot, that can grow in any direction.

## The system

- **Grid.** Modules sit on 52 mm squares, about 2 inches. The first grid was 40 mm and too
  small for real tools.
- **Joint.** Every outside wall has a half-bowtie socket at the middle of each square's edge,
  running top to bottom. A printed bowtie key drops into two facing sockets and locks the
  neighbours together.
- **Lift-out.** Every joint is vertical, so any module lifts straight up out of the field
  without disturbing the rest.
- **Labels.** Each module's front carries its name, and pocket floors carry the outline of the
  tool that lives there, so an empty outline shows what's out.

## The keys

The first test plate printed keys at three clearances. All three had play. The second round
added small crush ribs that squash as the key goes in, and the R1 key won. Eight blue R1 keys
followed, along with the first tray.

## The layout

The bench is nine squares wide and three deep, behind the soldering mat. It is laid out the
way a joint gets made: heat at the left near the iron, solder and flux in the middle, cleaning
at the right, with the alcohol at the far right away from heat. Tall modules stand at the back so
everything is in sight from the chair. A test checks the layout for overlaps and for those
rules. The first modules are printed; the rest follow as filament allows.
