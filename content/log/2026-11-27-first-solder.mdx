The [household robot](/builds/household-robot) has its wiring plan, and the first part of it is
soldered.

## The plan

- **Main fuse first.** A 10 A fuse sits a short lead from the battery's positive terminal, so
  everything after it is protected.
- **The current sensor before everything else.** Every amp in or out passes its shunt, so it
  counts charging as well as discharging and works as the robot's fuel gauge.
- **The charge socket joins before the main switch,** so the battery charges with the robot off.
  It has its own 3 A fuse, because the socket's leads are thinner than the main run.
- **Two rails after the switch.** One feeds a 12 V converter through a 5 A fuse for the servos.
  The other feeds the 5 V converter for the Pi.
- **One ground point,** soldered, so load current never runs through the sensor's ground.

There is no emergency stop in v1. The main switch is the one hardware cut, so v1 runs only with
someone watching. The e-stop arrives with the arm in v2.

## The method

This was the lab's first real soldering, so the method is written down step by step for the
joints still to come.

1. **Prep with the iron cold.** Strip 6 mm, twist the strands, and slide the heat-shrink on
   before the joint, 5 cm back. It can't go on afterwards.
2. **Tin both sides.** Lead-free solder at 370 °C. Coat the twisted wires, then the switch tab,
   in under 3 seconds so the switch's plastic doesn't soften.
3. **Join flat.** The two wires together are too thick for the tab's hole, so they lie flat
   along its face. Press the iron on them for about 3 seconds, then hold the wire still for 10
   while the solder sets.
4. **Shrink and test.** Slide the tube down over the joint, shrink it with the heat gun, then
   check the switch with the multimeter's beep test: a beep when on, silence when off.

Both leads passed.

## Next

The charge socket onto its fuse, the servo rail to the 12 V converter, the main fuse, the ground
point and the battery terminals. Then a continuity check of the whole harness before the battery
is ever connected.
