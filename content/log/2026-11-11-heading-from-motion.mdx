The second camera session moved off the puzzle mats onto a duct-tape rectangle on the
floor: 94.5 x 69.5 cm, nothing to slide, and the webcam at full frame.

## What the data said

- **The toy takes a fixed step.** Every button press moves Wall-e about 20 cm, even a
  0.3 second one; holding longer adds only a little. The fence assumed distance = speed x
  time and under-predicted short moves about four times, which is how he reached the
  edge. The fence model now has a fixed step per command.
- **Both buttons turn him right**, about 15 degrees forward and 11 degrees reverse, every
  press, with a few centimetres of sideways drift.
- **A timing bug, not a radio bug.** Several moves read as exactly 0.0 cm. The cause was
  the judge: when a frame wasn't tracked it quietly reused the previous pose. Now it waits
  for the robot to stop and needs three fresh fixes that agree.

## Stickers lost, motion won

Dark green on dark wood vanished. Blue on the head swung with the head. Blue on the base
hid under the overhanging head. Blue on the tracks gave headings that jumped by 110
degrees once he moved. Still, every one was steady to a degree; moving, none was usable.

Position, though, was steady to a millimetre throughout. So the heading now comes from
movement: after a forward step he faces the way he went, after a reverse step the
opposite way, corrected by the measured turn. The first move, with the heading unknown,
is only allowed if it's safe in any direction.

The next run: 30 moves, 29 tracked, 29 judged correctly, and the steering kept him in
the middle third of the arena with no fence refusals and no recovery needed.

## Also built

- **Recovery** inside the gate: a stray robot may be moved only if even the worst case
  ends further in than it is now.
- **Steering**: each move is picked at random among those predicted to end comfortably
  inside, so the data stays varied and the robot stays put.
- **Privacy held**: an empty-floor picture that caught a foot at the frame's edge was
  deleted at once and retaken.
