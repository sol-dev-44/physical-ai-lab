The plan from yesterday: better reach, two cameras, better data. All three moved today.

## Two cameras

- **Overhead:** a Logitech webcam about 50 to 70 cm above the mat, so the policy can find
  the Lego anywhere on it. Zoom and focus are set in Logitech's own app, because OpenCV
  can't control them on this Mac.
- **Wrist:** the small camera from the arm kit, mounted in the slot at the end of the
  follower, so the policy sees the grasp itself.

Both record at 640x480 and 30 frames a second. Camera numbers can change after a replug, so
a short probe runs before every recording.

## The lag

Both arms were recalibrated again. Then a gap showed up: on a quick
swing, the follower's shoulder lift trailed the leader by 100 to 155 degrees at the default
motor gain of 16. A policy trained on that learns a lagging arm.

Short arm-only tests compared gains:

| Gain | Result |
|---|---|
| 16 (default) | Shoulder lift up to 100 to 155 degrees behind on some runs |
| 32 | Mean lag about 3 degrees over a bowl episode, worst about 48 |
| 48 | No better than 32 on the quick swing |

Gain 32 is now the setting for recording and for running policies. The speed cap stays on.

## The task and the data

The task got a goal: pick up the red Lego from the mat and put it in a blue bowl. The bowl
stays in one place; the Lego moves every episode. Each episode is 30 seconds with a
15-second reset.

From episode 10 on, every demonstration starts and ends with the arm retracted at the same
rest pose. A policy then always starts from a pose it has seen, and learns to finish
by going home.

## A first look

A 5,000-step policy trained on the first nine episodes of the new dataset moved jerkily
and still never reached the Lego. Nine episodes and 5,000 steps is a smoke test, not a
result. The next step is more episodes and a much longer training run.
