Yesterday's policy grasped flat placements and missed the rest. The cheapest explanation is
the data, so today was more data, aimed at exactly what it missed.

## The batch

The dataset was copied first, then 25 episodes were added to it with the same settings as
before: gain 32, speed cap on, both cameras, 30-second episodes, rest to rest. The Lego's
placements followed a fixed schedule:

| Placement | Episodes |
|---|---|
| Flat, horizontal, spread across the mat | 6 |
| Vertical | 6 |
| Rotated to about 3, 6, 9 and 12 o'clock | 6 |
| Stacked on the other two blocks | 4 |
| Near the bowl or the mat's edges | 3 |

All 25 were clean demonstrations; none are left out. The dataset is now 65 episodes,
about 57,000 frames.

## Why the other blocks stay

Two other Lego blocks sit on the mat in every episode. ACT doesn't read the task's text, so
it can't be told "red". It goes for the red block because every demonstration does. The
other blocks are what make color the thing to learn, so they stay in the scene, flat, moved
to a new spot each episode.

## The retrain

A fresh 50,000-step run on episodes 1 to 64, from scratch, with the same settings as the
first. On the Mac that's about eight hours, so it runs in the background, saving a
checkpoint every 5,000 steps.

## How it will be judged

Not by training loss. The 30,000-step and 50,000-step checkpoints each get about 10 trials
on the same fixed placements, with the Lego reset by hand between runs, and the one with
more successful grasps is kept. Every trial goes into the per-trial table in
`experiments/exp002-so-arm-bench/trials.md`: checkpoint, settings, placement, outcome and
attempts.
