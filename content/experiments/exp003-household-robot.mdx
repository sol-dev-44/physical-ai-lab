## Why

Experiments 0 to 2 ran on a desk: a toy behind an IR blaster, then a fixed arm. A robot that
drives around a home is a harder safety problem. It moves where people and pets are, it runs on a
battery, and it is controlled over Wi-Fi, which drops.

This experiment builds that robot from the ground up and tests whether the lab's harness holds up
on it: one gated path to the motors, every command logged, and a robot that stops whenever
anything goes wrong. The hardware itself is written up on the
[household robot build page](/builds/household-robot), with drawings and every part.

## Setup

- **Drive.** Two bus servos in wheel mode on printed hubs and TPU tires, and a rear caster.
- **Compute.** A Raspberry Pi 5 on the robot runs the motors, sensors and the safety gate. Heavier
  work (speech, recognition, policies) runs on the lab machine over Wi-Fi.
- **Power.** A 12.8 V LiFePO4 pack through a main switch and 10 A fuse, then a current sensor
  that counts charging and discharging, then a 12 V rail for the servos and a 5 V rail for the Pi.
- **Control.** The robot serves its own phone app on the home network. Driving is a dead-man: the
  phone holds a 300 ms lease on the wheels and renews it while the stick is held. `base.stop` is a
  tool the gate never refuses.

<Callout>
  Nothing runs against hardware yet. The control app drives simulated wheels, and the wiring is
  being soldered. No results are published until the first real-wheel test has run through the
  harness and been logged.
</Callout>

## What it will measure

Each phase has a pass mark set before it runs. The four below come first, and all of them are
about stopping.

| Phase | Test | Pass mark |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0. Bench | Drive two servos from the phone app and an Xbox controller | Releasing the stick, closing the tab or dropping Wi-Fi stops the wheels, over 10 minutes of driving |
| 1. Chassis | Teleop over Wi-Fi on the built robot | 10 minutes of driving; the dead-man stops it every time |
| 2. Interlocks | Bumpers, forward distance sensors, cliff sensors, a speed cap of about 0.3 m/s | Stops before a box 20/20 and at a test edge 10/10 |
| 4. Remote | Driving over a private VPN | Stops within 300 ms when the link drops |

Later phases add voice, mapping, household rules enforced by the gate (no-go zones, quiet hours),
recognising who it is talking to, and a vision-language-action policy trained on its own driving.
A second version adds an arm to put toys away.

## Open risks

- **A servo that keeps turning.** In wheel mode, a bus servo holds its last speed. If the Pi's
  software dies mid-command, the wheels may keep going. Phase 0 tests this before the robot is on
  the floor.
- **No e-stop in v1.** The main switch is the only hardware cut, so v1 runs only with someone
  watching. The e-stop comes with the arm.
- **Wheel mounts under load.** The first mounts rolled over under the robot's weight. The one-piece
  module that replaced them passed a hands-on load check, and a bearing on each wheel comes before
  the arm.
