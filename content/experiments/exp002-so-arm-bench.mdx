## Why

Experiments 0 and 1 proved the harness on a toy: one gated path to hardware, one event log, and a camera
that judges the result. A toy with two buttons cannot learn anything, though. This experiment moves to a
real manipulator and a learned policy, and asks the question every robot-learning project runs into:
when the policy fails, is more data the fix, and which data?

The task is deliberately small. A red Lego block sits somewhere on a mat, two other blocks sit nearby as
distractors, and the arm has to put the red one in a bowl and return to rest.

## Setup

- **Arms.** A Seeed SO-ARM101 Pro pair. The black leader arm is moved by hand and only read; the white
  follower arm copies it during teleoperation and runs the policy afterwards.
- **Cameras.** An overhead webcam and a wrist camera, both recorded at 640x480 and 30 fps.
- **Software.** LeRobot 0.7.1 (Seeed's fork), kept outside the lab's own Python workspace.
- **Policy.** ACT (Action Chunking with Transformers), about 52M parameters. ACT does not read the task
  text, so the distractor blocks are what teach it to pick by colour.
- **Speed cap.** Every follower step is capped (`max_relative_target=5`) during teleoperation and rollouts.
  The policy often asks for bigger steps, and the logs record each clamp.

<Callout>
  The arm does not run through the lab's safety gate yet. Teleoperation and rollouts use LeRobot directly,
  with the speed cap as the only limit. Moving rollouts onto the harness (logged, gated and scored like
  Experiments 0 and 1) is the next piece of harness work.
</Callout>

## Data

Every episode is 30 seconds with a 15 second reset, and starts and ends with the arm at rest.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Dataset | `exp002-bowl-p32`, 65 episodes, 57,252 frames at 30 fps |
| Bowl batch | 40 episodes, the Lego moved each time (episode 0 excluded as a failure) |
| Orientation batch | 25 episodes on 15 November: 6 flat, 6 vertical, 6 rotated, 4 stacked, 3 near the bowl or an edge |
| Follower gain | 32. At the default 16 the shoulder lagged the leader by up to 100 to 155 degrees; at 32 the mean lag was about 3 |

The orientation batch exists because of what the first policy got wrong.

## First policy and what it did

The first policy trained for 50,000 steps on episodes 1 to 39, on the Mac's GPU (MPS), in 7 hours 44
minutes. These nine rollouts were exploratory, run to learn what to test next. They were not a fixed
protocol, so they are not a success rate.

| # | Settings | Lego placement | Outcome | Attempts | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | default | not recorded | grasped after a retry | 2 | bumped it, regrasped, into the bowl |
| 2 | default | flat, horizontal | missed | about 2 | |
| 3 | default | flat, horizontal | grasped first try | 1 | |
| 4 | default | vertical | missed | 3 | |
| 5 | default | stacked on the other blocks | grasped first try | 1 | |
| 6 | default | rotated to 12 o'clock | missed | 3 | the wrist did not rotate |
| 7 | default | flat, horizontal | missed | about 4 | overshot left twice |
| 8 | 10-step chunks | not recorded | missed | 0 | barely moved, shook |
| 9 | temporal ensembling | not recorded | missed | 3 | held 30 Hz, wrist under-rolled |

"Default" is ACT's 100-action chunks. Where the placement was recorded, the grasps came on flat and stacked blocks. Every vertical and
rotated block was missed, and those placements were rare in the training data.

## What it taught

- **The misses look like a data problem.** The policy works on placements it saw often and fails where the
  wrist has to roll. Rollout settings change how a policy runs, not what it learned, so better grasps need
  more of the right data and a retrain.
- **Smoother is not better.** Temporal ensembling held the control loop at 30 Hz (100-action chunks dip to
  about 16 Hz) and moved more smoothly, but grasped nothing.
- **Inference is fast enough.** About 50 to 60 ms per step on the Mac, with spikes near 200 ms.
- **The jerkiness has a likely cause.** The speed cap clamps many steps. A higher cap has not been tested.

## Next

1. A second policy, trained from scratch on episodes 1 to 64 (with the orientation batch), is training now.
2. Compare its checkpoints on the same fixed placements, about 10 trials each, and keep the one with more
   successful grasps, not the one with the lowest training loss.
3. Keep adding batches toward 100 episodes, then retrain and compare again.
4. Log rollouts through the harness, so results on this page are computed from runs instead of typed in.
